BUILT FOR LOCAL GOVERNMENT

I'm Paul, but most people know me as Monsai. I'm a full-stack engineer and web designer based in Baybay City, Philippines, working mainly with React on the frontend and Laravel on the backend.

Most of my recent work has been building systems for local government: tracking documents, handling emergency reports and alerts, running port ticketing, and registering pets city-wide. Real offices use these every day, so they need to be dependable and easy for non-technical staff to pick up.

Before that, I built authentication tooling and a virtual touring platform, and designed and developed websites for travel and pest control businesses, so I'm comfortable moving between backend systems work and front-facing design.

I hold a BS in Information Technology from Western Leyte College of Ormoc, where my thesis project was an online business permit system. That was my first time building software for local government, and now it's most of my day job.

(02) Systems

Civic systems, in production

Systems I've built for Baybay City's local government. Each one replaced a manual, paper-based process with something residents and staff actually rely on.

SCROLL
WEB APPIN USE

Document Tracking System

Staff log official documents here and route them between offices. Every document gets a tracking number (like DTS-2026-000005), the dashboard groups everything by age so stuck paperwork is easy to spot, and offices file delay memos when something runs late. It replaced the old manual logbooks.

ReactLaravelMySQL
ANDROID APPIN USE

PPA Baybay POS

The app gate staff at the Philippine Ports Authority's Baybay port use to issue port user's passes. They pick a vehicle (₱12) or passenger (₱6) pass, the app works out the VAT and prints the receipt on the spot, and at the end of the shift it prints a summary so the day's sales add up without manual counting.

KotlinLaravelAndroidReceipt printing
WEB + MOBILEIN USE

BâyWatch

Emergency reporting and official alerts for Baybay City. Residents in all 92 barangays can send 9 kinds of reports, like floods, fires and accidents, which go to CDRRMO and the 8 offices that respond. CDRRMO sends official alerts back out in 3 languages, and residents can answer "I'm safe" or "I need help".
